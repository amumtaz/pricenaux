The Multichannel Pricing Challenge

The same product can behave differently on Amazon and Shopify.

Amazon and Shopify expose merchants to different pricing signals. Shopify can provide first-party order and product-performance evidence, while Amazon adds marketplace competition, seller activity, Buy Box context, and broader competitive pressure.

Different Demand Signals

A product may perform differently across channels because the buying environment, customer journey, and competitive context are different.

Different Competitive Pressure

Amazon marketplace activity can create pricing pressure that may not exist in the same way on a merchant's Shopify storefront.

Different Commercial Economics

Margin considerations, positioning, channel costs, and commercial objectives can differ even when the underlying product is the same.

One Product. Two Selling Environments.

Connected intelligence does not mean identical pricing.

A connected commercial view helps merchants understand how a product is performing across channels without assuming Amazon and Shopify should always produce the same pricing decision.

The goal is coordination, not forced uniformity. A product can face different demand, competition, margin economics, and strategic objectives on Amazon and Shopify. Pricenaux helps merchants understand those differences while maintaining a connected commercial view.

Margin & Channel Economics

A lower marketplace price does not automatically define the right storefront price.

Channel economics can differ. Pricing decisions should account for demand, product performance, competitive position, margin considerations, and commercial objectives rather than assuming one channel should mechanically follow the other.

Cross-channel awareness should protect commercial logic. A pricing move on Amazon may be appropriate for the marketplace environment without automatically becoming the correct Shopify response.
