The Launch Pricing Problem

You Need a Price Before You Have the Data

Historical performance can become an important source of pricing intelligence over time. But a new seller or newly introduced product does not yet have enough product-level order history to rely on it.

That creates a practical cold-start problem: the merchant must make an important pricing decision before meaningful product performance has accumulated.

Launch Pricing helps bridge that gap by placing the product in its available market and competitive context before recommending a starting position.

Who It Is For

Two Different Launches. The Same Pricing Question.

Launch Pricing supports both merchants entering e-commerce for the first time and established sellers introducing products that do not yet have meaningful sales history.

Launch vs. Performance-Driven Pricing

The Right Pricing Approach Depends on the Data You Have

New / Low Data

Launch Pricing

Best for: New sellers, new products, and low-data products.

Primary challenge: Meaningful product-level sales history is not yet available.

Focus: Market context, competitive positioning, and the seller's selected pricing scenario.

Purpose: Establish an informed starting position.

Performance Aware

Performance-Driven Pricing

Best for: Products with meaningful historical sales information.

Primary advantage: Actual product behavior can contribute additional evidence.

Focus: Product performance, demand sensitivity, market context, and commercial constraints.

Purpose: Refine pricing using observed performance.

Protect the Starting Position

Launching Does Not Mean Racing to the Lowest Price

Starting below the market simply because a competitor is cheaper can sacrifice margin before the seller has learned how the product will actually perform. Starting too high can create a different challenge.

Launch Pricing frames the decision around the seller's objective, intended position, and available market context rather than automatically following a competitor.
